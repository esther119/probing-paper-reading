SEAL— Steerable Reasoning Calibration —

Chen et al. · COLM MMXXV

Reasoning models over-think — they re-check and restart far more than needed. SEAL finds a single "stay-on-task" direction inside the model's activations and nudges every thought toward it. No retraining: H′ = H + α·S. This walkthrough builds that idea from the ground up.

Paper: SEAL: Steerable Reasoning Calibration of Large Language Models for Free · [PDF]

What this paper is about

A reasoning model already "knows" the difference between productive work and spinning its wheels — those live in different directions of its activation space. SEAL measures that direction once, then leans the model toward it at every thought boundary.

The method splits a model's chain-of-thought into three kinds of "thoughts" — execution (actual solving), reflection (re-checking), and transition (switching approach) — and shows reflection & transition are mostly redundant. The whole pipeline, at a glance:

Thoughts are geometrically distinguishable (§3)
Read the hidden state at each thought's ending \n\n. In deep layers, execution thoughts separate cleanly from reflection/transition — so the "type" of thinking is readable.
▼
Build the steering vector S — offline, once
Average those hidden states by category and subtract: S = H̄E − H̄RT — a single frozen vector pointing from "over-thinking" toward "execution."
▼
Steer at decode — every query
At every \n\n, add it in: H′ = H + α·S. Each nudge biases the next thought toward execution → shorter, more accurate reasoning.
The payoff. Up to +11% accuracy while cutting 12–50% of reasoning tokens — entirely from that one additive nudge, with no fine-tuning.

Lesson 1 — Reasoning patterns live in different places

Before you can steer anything, you must prove the "kind" of thinking is encoded somewhere readable. SEAL shows it's encoded in the latent space — and that it gets clearer the deeper you look.

You can't spot over-thinking from the words: a reflection thought says wildly different things per question. So SEAL looks one level down, at the hidden state — the activation vector the model holds at a layer. The bet: the type of reasoning shows up as a region in that space.

The probe. Run DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on Math-500. For each thought, grab the hidden state at the \n\n that ends it (layer i), project to 2-D with t-SNE, and look. Drag the slider to sweep layers 1→28.

The three findings

  • Execution stands apart. At deep layers it barely overlaps the others — at layer 20 it's almost fully separated. That's where SEAL later intervenes.
  • Depth sharpens it. Early layers can't tell types apart; deep layers can — shallow = surface features, deep = abstract meaning.
  • Reflection ≈ Transition. They stay close to each other even as both pull from execution, because both mean "reconsider."
Takeaway: separable means measurable. Because the types occupy distinct regions, you can compute a direction pointing from "over-thinking" toward "execution" — and then push the model along it. That direction is S.

Lesson 2 — The nudge: H′ = H + α·S

SEAL finds that "stay-on-task" direction and, at decode time, nudges the activations along it. The nudge is exactly this equation.

H′ = H + α · S
Hthe hidden state — the model's "thought" as numbers, before steering
H′the steered hidden state — what flows on to the next layers and shapes the next token
α (alpha)a single strength knob. The paper uses α = 1.0; α = 0 means "do nothing"
Sthe steering vector — the fixed "be execution, not reflection/transition" direction from Lesson 1

Where S comes from

Take the same \n\n hidden states, average within each category, then subtract:

S = H̄E − H̄RT   ( H̄RT = mean of Reflection ∪ Transition )

S points from the average over-thinking thought toward the average on-task thought. Adding it to H drags the state toward execution.

Steering in activation space — drag α

α 1.00
execution region reflection / transition H′ (steered) S direction
Takeaway: it's just vector addition. Scale the "stay-on-task" direction S by α and add it to the current thought H. With α≈1 the state crosses out of the over-thinking cluster into execution — biasing what the model writes next.

Lesson 3 — How S is actually built

The confusing part is "hidden state" and "averaging." Let's make both concrete — first the pipeline, then the same thing with real numbers.

The extraction, step by step

Each colored grid below is one vector of numbers — a cartoon of d≈3584 real values, shown as 10 cells.

One token does both jobs: the \n\n that ends each thought

"First token \n\n" sounds like the start, but it's the end: a thought is the content between two \n\n separators, so the "first \n\n" is the separator right after the thought. Crucially, the same token read offline is the token steered at decode. SEAL only ever touches these boundary tokens — never the words.

thought j"… 7 × 6 = 42"
read + steer\n\n
thought j+1"Wait, let me check"
read + steer\n\n
thought j+2"Alternatively …"

SEAL acts only on the \n\n boundary tokens (layer 20) — every one of them. The thought text is never modified.

① Offline — readAt each \n\n, record the hidden state. Pile them up → average → S. No steering here.
② Decode — writeAt each \n\n, overwrite it: H′ = H + α·S. This is the steering.

A "query" = one question from the eval/test set (Math-500, GSM8k, …) or a real user — not a build-set problem. Different questions, but the numbers added are identical every time. That is "one frozen vector."
❓"What is 25 × 16?" …thought… +S.5.2.5 …thought… +S.5.2.5
❓"Is 91 prime?" …thought… +S.5.2.5
❓"Solve x² = 49" …thought… +S.5.2.5 …thought… +S.5.2.5
…and every future question…
The bathroom-scale version. Your scale reads 0.5 kg heavy. You measure that error once and write "−0.5 kg" on a sticky note — that's your frozen value. Then every weigh-in forever, you subtract the same 0.5 kg. S is that sticky note — except it's 3 (really 3584) numbers, applied at every \n\n.

Two different datasets. S is built on the MATH train split and then steers held-out test questions — the build set and the eval set never overlap, so the reported scores aren't inflated.
Takeaway: offline = calibrate an instrument once in the lab (watch many runs, bottle one setting S); decode = apply that fixed setting to every new measurement in the field. Calibrate once, apply forever.

Lesson 5 — Experiments & ablations

Does it work, and is every design choice load-bearing? The setup, the headline results, and the ablation that justifies the formula.

Two arguments the experiments make: latent > token space (Logits Penalty only kills specific tokens; the model just rephrases its over-thinking, so it even increases reworded reflection steps — SEAL suppresses the concept), and S transfers (a MATH-built vector still helps on GSM8k and on LiveCodeBench code).

Ablation that justifies S = H̄E − H̄RT

Rebuild S to weaken different categories and watch accuracy (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B). 👉 Click the variants:

Accuracy@1 across three datasets · DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B

What it proves: weakening execution collapses accuracy (it's the real work); weakening reflection or transition alone helps a bit; weakening both helps most — so the optimal vector subtracts the combined reflection+transition mean, i.e. H̄RT.

Two more axes

  • Which layer? S extracted from each layer is tested; mid-to-late layers win (concepts live there) → layer 20.
  • How hard (α)? Larger α reliably cuts reflection/transition, but driving them to zero hurts — some rechecking helps. α = 1.0 is consistently strong, so it's fixed everywhere.
Putting it together: §3 proves "solving" and "second-guessing" sit in different regions; §4 measures the direction between them (S) and leans every thought boundary toward "keep working"; §5 confirms each choice — subtract both over-thinking types, steer a middle layer, with a moderate α. Steering, not retraining.
